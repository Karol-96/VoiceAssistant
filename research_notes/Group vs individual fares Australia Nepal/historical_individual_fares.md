# Historical individual (non-group) economy airfares: Australia → Kathmandu (KTM). Fares by city, seasonality, festival and holiday premiums, lead time

## 1. Typical and cheapest return economy fares (AUD) from each Australian city

### Takeaway
In dated 2025–2026 observations, the cheapest individual return economy fares to KTM were about AUD 850–900. These were Malaysia Airlines promotions via Kuala Lumpur from Perth and Brisbane, and Melbourne monthly lows for August 2026. Typical return fares were about AUD 1,000–1,300 from Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Adelaide and Darwin, AUD 850–1,050 from Perth, about AUD 1,300–1,400 from the Gold Coast, and AUD 1,370–1,850 from Canberra. No Australian city has a non-stop flight to KTM.

#### Conventions used throughout (read first)
- **Fetch limits.** `www.kayak.com.au` and `theannapurnaexpress.com` failed DNS resolution in this environment because they are outside the network allowlist. Following the environment's instruction, I stopped fetching pages. Every figure below comes from search-result snippets and summaries returned on **20 Nov 2026**. Capture dates appear only where the snippet showed them.
- **Tags.**
  - **[Q]**: a quoted fare for stated travel dates, or a dated airline or deal listing. This is the closest thing to a price that was bookable at that time.
  - **[E]**: an aggregator "from" or best-found estimate. These are teaser lows, often with no dates.
  - **[A]**: an aggregator monthly average or median.
  - **HIST (date)**: a dated or historical observation.
  - **CUR**: a current listing seen on 20 Nov 2026, with no capture date shown.
- **Currency.**
  - "$" on Australian-domain sites (kayak.com.au, cheapflights.com.au, momondo.com.au, iwantthatflight.com.au, skyscanner.com.au, expedia.com.au) is treated as AUD.
  - "$" on .com sites is treated as USD unless stated otherwise.
  - USD→AUD uses **1.4545**, from FRED H.10 (0.6875 USD per AUD on 6 Nov 2026, down from 0.7011 on 4 Nov) — [FRED/ALFRED DEXUSAL](https://alfred.stlouisfed.org/series?seid=DEXUSAL). Trading Economics had the AUD at about US$0.68 around 10 Nov 2026, a seven-month low — [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/australia/currency/news/600183).
  - Other conversions use indicative cross rates that I did **not** verify in this session: 1 GBP ≈ 1.92 AUD, 1 EUR ≈ 1.68, 1 SGD ≈ 1.13, 1 CAD ≈ 1.05, 1 NZD ≈ 0.86, 1 INR ≈ 0.0165, 1 PKR ≈ 0.0052, 1 THB ≈ 0.044. Treat converted values as accurate to within ±5–8%.

## 2. Cheapest and most expensive months, and the size of the Dashain/Tihar (late Sep–Nov) and mid-Dec–Jan premiums

### Takeaway
The cheapest months are July–August, with a second low in February–March. The most expensive outbound months are October, which covers Dashain and Tihar, and December. On the return leg (KTM→Australia), January is the peak. Source-reported averages put October about 26–34% above the cheapest month, about AUD 250–450 on a typical fare. Dated quotes show festival or holiday dates up to +60% over cheap dates. Guide ranges for festival months (AUD 1,400–1,900) sit 57–123% above the cheapest promotional fares.

## 3. Lead time: "best time to book" and price-trend data for these routes

### Takeaway
Route-level data for Australia–KTM points to booking about **2–6 months ahead** for the lowest fares. Kayak AU puts the absolute cheapest for BNE→KTM at 23 weeks and below-average fares at 12 weeks. Cheapflights puts it at about 87–88 days, and Skyscanner AU's all-international average is 22 weeks. Fares typically climb inside the final **two months**, with about 10–20% savings quoted for booking 2–3.5 months ahead rather than last minute. No source shows that booking 10–12 months ahead beats booking 3–6 months ahead for off-peak travel.

### Cited Findings
- **Kayak AU, BNE→KTM.** "Book around 12 weeks before departure" for a below-average fare. "For the absolute cheapest price, our data suggests you should book **23 weeks** before departure" — [Kayak AU BNE](https://kayak.com.au/flight-routes/Brisbane-BNE/Kathmandu-Tribhuvan-KTM).

### Inferences
- **10–12 months ahead.** No route data shows that this window is cheapest for off-peak travel. The most bullish source, Trabber, says about 8.5 months; Kayak AU's cheapest point is about 5.3 months (23 weeks).
  - For **festival dates**, very early booking mainly protects access to the lower fare classes, because seats "book out months in advance".
  - So it should still be advisable for Dashain, Tihar and Christmas departures. This is consistent with ShopBack's 12–20 weeks or more for peak periods.
- **3–6 months ahead.** This is the sweet spot in most datasets: Kayak AU at 12–23 weeks, Kayak UK at 14 weeks, Cheapflights at about 12.5 weeks, Expedia at 3 months, and Skyscanner's 22-week average. The Malaysia Airlines promotions were also sold about 6–7 months out.
- **Under 2 months.** Prices "take an upward turn", according to Skyscanner. The only route-specific quantification is Kayak UK's BNE figure of about 20% more for last-minute than for booking at 14 weeks. Expedia puts it at about 10% for booking 2 months ahead.
  - On an AUD 1,100 off-peak fare, that implies roughly **+AUD 110–220** for booking late.
  - For peak dates the climb is likely steeper and availability is the binding constraint, but I found no AUD quantification.
- Kayak AU SYD's "4 weeks" and Expedia's "4–6 weeks" suggest that below-average fares are still findable about a month out for *off-peak* dates. They should not be read as advice for festival travel.

## 5. One-way vs return: are one-ways typical, and how do they compare?

### Takeaway
One-way fares are widely sold on these routes and typically cost **about 46–53% of the matching return**, so there is no meaningful round-trip discount. Two one-ways cost roughly the same as a return, and aggregators actively promote mixing one-ways.

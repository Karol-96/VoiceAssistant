# Diaspora market evidence: how Nepali-Australians buy festival flights home (Dashain, Tihar, December)

*Compiled 20 November 2026. Dashain 2026 ran 11–25 October and Tihar 8–11 November 2026 ([White Tiger Holidays](https://whitetigerholidays.com/dashain-tihar-2026-holiday-packages/)).*

*Method and limits.* WebFetch failed for every external host tried in this session: abs.gov.au, homeaffairs.gov.au, myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com, dawn.com, skytrips.com.au and tripadvisor.com all returned DNS "ENOTFOUND", meaning they are outside the session's allowed network. The search tool also refuses reddit.com outright ("The following domains are not accessible to our user agent: ['reddit.com']"). **Every finding below therefore comes from search-result snippets and the search tool's summaries, not from reading full pages.** Text in quotation marks appeared as quoted text in those snippets. Dates written "≈" were inferred from the search tool's "N days old" page metadata. Labels used: **CURRENT** means the Dashain/Tihar 2026 season or a November 2026 snapshot. **HISTORICAL** means 2022 to mid-2026. **PRE-2022** means older context.

*Exchange rates.* These are approximate and were not checked live against Nepal Rastra Bank.
- **2024 NPR figures:** AUD 1 ≈ NPR 88. This is consistent with an undated Google Finance "previous close" of 87.96 that appeared in search ([Google Finance](https://www.google.com/finance/quote/AUD-NPR?hl=en)).
- **2026 NPR figures:** shown as a range, AUD 1 = NPR 106 (low AUD value) to NPR 88 (high AUD value). Rates in the snippets conflicted and none was dated: Wise showed 105.7 and 108.81, Google Finance 87.96 ([Wise](https://wise.com/au/currency-converter/aud-to-npr-rate); [Wise US](https://wise.com/us/currency-converter/aud-to-npr-rate)).
- **Other currencies:** the compiler's own approximations, so AUD equivalents are indicative only. AUD 1 ≈ USD 0.66 ≈ AED 2.42 (the AED is pegged at 3.6725 per USD) ≈ MYR 2.8 ≈ JPY 97 ≈ GBP 0.49.

## 1. Which agencies and community channels serve Nepali-Australians, and what fares do they advertise (Dashain/Tihar specials, group fares, block seats)?

### Takeaway
Several agencies that are Nepali-run or focused on Nepal market Australia–Kathmandu flights online, covering Sydney, Perth, Adelaide, Canberra and multi-city lists that include Darwin, Hobart and Cairns. Their published fares are generic "starting from" prices; for example, Hifi Travels shows AUD 450–700 from ten Australian cities (page ≈ July 2026). **No page from an Australian-Nepali agency was found advertising a Dashain/Tihar special, a group fare, block seats or a "fly together" package.** The only festival-branded fare offer found is an airline promotion sold out of Kathmandu: Qatar Airways' Dashain 2026 sale, which covers Kathmandu to the Americas, Europe and Africa, not Australia.

### Inferences
- Hifi Travels' AUD 450–700 "starting price" is a lead-in fare, probably one-way or off-peak. It sits well below every December return snapshot found: Thai Airways from AUD 1,680 and Google Flights A$1,600–2,600 (see Q3). Agency web pricing therefore does not show what festival travellers actually pay.
- Australian-Nepali agencies appear to sell festival travel through Facebook, WhatsApp and word of mouth rather than on indexed web pages. Hamrofind names local Nepali Facebook groups as the community's announcement channel. This is unverified because Facebook is not indexed.
- The only festival-branded fare product in the market is airline-led, sold out of Kathmandu, and aimed at outbound Nepali leisure travel. Qatar's blackout on inbound-to-Kathmandu travel for 1–8 Nov 2026 suggests airlines protect the Tihar homecoming peak rather than discount it.
- No group or block-seat product targeting Nepali-Australians going home was found.

### Gaps
- No agency site could be opened to read festival fare pages, flyers or terms; DNS was blocked for skytrips.com.au and others. Exact advertised prices, dates and fare types for Skytrips, Hifi Travels, Holidayz Nepal, Doko Tours and HopNepal are unknown.
- No Nepali agency based in Melbourne, Brisbane, Adelaide or Darwin surfaced by name with fares.
- No advertised group fares, block seats or "fly together" deals were found for any Australian city.
- No adverts from Australian-Nepali media (Nepal Samachar Patra, Nepalese Voice) were surfaced.

## 2. Are there documented examples of organised group travel from Australia to Nepal (associations, NRNA, students, pilgrimages, trekking, weddings), and what did they pay?

### Takeaway
No documented, priced example of organised group travel from Australia to Nepal was found, whether by an association, NRNA, students, pilgrims, a wedding party or a festival group. The visible evidence shows individual or family trips home, and community Dashain events held in Australia. The group products that do exist (Dashain festival tours, pilgrimage circuits) are sold by Nepal- or India-based operators to foreign tourists, priced in USD or INR, and exclude international flights.

### Cited Findings

No travel logistics or fares for an Australian delegation surfaced.

### Inferences
- If group travel home happens, it is informal: families or friend groups coordinating, or an agency holding a handful of seats. It leaves no indexed public footprint, which is consistent with Q1's finding that agencies market through social channels.
- NRNA global conferences (2023, and the proposed 2027 Knowledge Conference) are the most plausible precedent for association-organised group travel from Australia. They fall outside festival dates, however, and no fare evidence surfaced.
- Some family reunions happen in the reverse direction, with parents flying Kathmandu → Australia, so festival demand is not only Australia → Nepal.

### Gaps
- No group-trip announcements were found from NRNA Australia, NCAWA or other associations, nor any wedding-party block bookings, student-group trips or pilgrimage groups departing Australia, with or without prices.
- Facebook and WhatsApp groups, where such trips would likely be organised, are not indexed or accessible.

## 3. What do Nepali and Australian-Nepali media report about festival-season airfares (hikes, sell-outs, comparisons for Australia, Gulf and Malaysia → Kathmandu)?

### Takeaway
- **Australia route:** the best-documented spike is November 2024. Kathmandu–Sydney went from a "normal" Rs85,000 (≈AUD 966) to Rs114,000–217,000 (≈AUD 1,295–2,466), an increase of 34% to 155%. This combined post-Tihar and student demand with the Kathmandu airport's night closure.
- **Gulf and Malaysia:** bigger multiples. Dubai→Kathmandu was about +200% in October 2025, Kathmandu→Dubai 2.3–4.9× in late 2024, and Kathmandu→Kuala Lumpur 4.25× in April 2026 (fuel crisis, not a festival).
- **Dashain/Tihar 2026:** Saudi flight cancellations pushed one-way fares to Rs100,000 (≈AUD 940–1,140).
- **Domestic Nepal:** fares rise 68% to 100% at Dashain.
- **Gap:** no outlet was found quantifying a Dashain 2025 or 2026 spike specifically on Australia–Nepal routes. Current aggregator snapshots show December SYD–KTM fares about 20–65% above February/autumn levels.

### Cited Findings

  - Between 8 and 25 Oct 2026, about 45% of scheduled flights from Saudi airports were cancelled, reaching 86% at Riyadh's King Khalid airport.

**HISTORICAL (2022 – mid-2026)**
- **Kathmandu → Sydney spike, Kathmandu Post 29 Oct 2024:**
  - "Sydney, which would have cost Rs85,000 on normal days, will now cost Rs217,000 (for the highest priced tickets) and Rs114,000 (lowest)." That is ≈AUD 966 normal versus ≈AUD 1,295–2,466, or +34% to +155%.

### Inferences
- **Order-of-magnitude peak premiums:**
  - Australia ↔ Nepal: about 1.3–2.6× normal in a stressed peak (Nov 2024), and about 1.2–1.65× December over February in 2026 snapshots.
  - Gulf ↔ Nepal: about 2–5× in festival or disruption peaks.
  - Kuala Lumpur ↔ Kathmandu: 4.25× in the April 2026 fuel shock.
  - Domestic Nepal: about 1.7–2× at Dashain.
  - The Australia route appears less extreme than Gulf routes in percentage terms but is much higher in absolute AUD.
- **Peaks run in both directions for Nepali-Australians:** Australia → Kathmandu before Dashain/Tihar (late September to early November) and in December–January; Kathmandu → Australia after Tihar (November) and before the February and July university intakes. The Nov 2024 Kathmandu Post figures are for Kathmandu → Sydney.
- **2026 base level:** festival premiums sit on top of a fuel-inflated base. Jet fuel is still about 75% above pre-war levels, and hedges are expiring.
- **What drives the Gulf spikes:** they are shaped by mass worker movement and employer ticket allowances (for example, a Rs42,000 allowance against an Rs82,000 ticket). This is a different buyer profile from Australian-Nepali families and students.

### Inferences
- The consensus heuristic is "book 2–3+ months ahead". Dashain moves with the lunar calendar (11–25 Oct in 2026), so the cheapest window closes roughly in July–August for October travel. That timing favours pre-commitment or pooled-booking mechanisms.
- A meaningful share of Nepali-Australians, especially students and recent arrivals, celebrate in Australia rather than fly home. Taking a day of annual leave and the community events point to this. Festival-travel demand is therefore a subset of the diaspora, concentrated among people who can take two to four weeks off.

- No dated first-hand fare reports ("we paid $X for Dashain") from Reddit, Facebook or Tripadvisor; all three are inaccessible in this environment.
- No SBS Nepali segment with fare figures surfaced. Nepali-language audio content is not searchable via snippets.

## 5. How large is the demand (ABS 2021 Census, later estimates, trips home), and is anyone running group-buying or demand-aggregation for this market?

### Takeaway
- **Population:** the 2021 Census counted about 122,500 Nepal-born residents (+124% since 2016), 53% in NSW. Home Affairs estimated 197,800 by June 2024 and the ABS about 213,600 by June 2025.
- **Students:** about 71,000 Nepali nationals were studying in Australia in 2025.
- **Travel volume:** Nepal recorded 37,550 arrivals from Australia in 2025, Australian passport holders only.
- **Trips home:** no data on trips home per year was found.
- **Group buying:** no group-buying or demand-aggregation model for Nepali diaspora flights was found in Australia, the UK, Japan or the Gulf. The closest analogues are Bangladesh's Hometown (GoZayaan), a migrant-worker booking app built around booking months ahead for Eid, and Nepal Airlines' quota of ten discounted seats per flight for migrant workers.

### Cited Findings
**ABS Census 2021: Nepal-born residents**
- **National:** 122,506 ([Wikipedia, citing ABS](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Nepalese_Australian)). ### Inferences
- **State counts derived from cited figures:**
  - Greater Melbourne ≈ 18,522 (20,154 − 1,632).
  - Queensland ≈ 10,800 (8.8% × 122,506).
  - Tasmania ≈ 6,100 and NT ≈ 2,450 (Home Affairs 5% and 2% shares).
  - The cited counts are internally consistent: NSW 64,946 = 53.0% and ACT 5,689 ≈ 4.6% of 122,506.
- **Growth:** the Nepal-born population grew from about 122.5k (Census, Aug 2021) to about 213.6k (ERP, June 2025). These are different measures, since the ERP adjusts for undercount, but the base is clearly far larger than the 2021 Census suggests. Demand outside Sydney (Melbourne, Brisbane, Adelaide, Hobart, Canberra, Darwin) is material: about 47% of the Census population.
- **Students:** about a third of the community (≈71k of ≈214k) are students. They face the most price sensitivity and timing conflicts (semester dates, the February and July intakes) and are the likeliest early adopters of pooled fares.
- **NTB arrivals understate the flow:** the 37,550 Australian arrivals in 2025 is a floor for Nepal-bound travel from Australia. It counts foreign passport holders only, so it excludes Nepali-passport holders (students, temporary residents and many permanent residents) who enter Nepal as citizens. This is the compiler's reading of how NTB tourist statistics work and was not verified in-session.
- **Market gap:** no aggregator exists for Nepali-diaspora festival travel. Airline programmes are narrow worker quotas, and the Qatar promotion is outbound-only. The Hometown case shows that "book months ahead for the festival" is a proven value proposition for South Asian migrant travellers.

### Gaps
- Exact 2021 Census counts for Queensland, WA, Tasmania and the NT were not captured, because the QuickStats pages could not be fetched.
- The ABS June 2025 ERP page has an internal conflict (213.58k versus "163,000") that was not resolved.
- No data on trips home per year or per person was found. ABS overseas arrivals and departures by country for Australian residents travelling to Nepal did not surface.
- No evidence was found of anyone, in Australia or elsewhere, running a group-buying model for Nepali festival travel. This may reflect search limits, since Facebook-based group-buy schemes would not be indexed.

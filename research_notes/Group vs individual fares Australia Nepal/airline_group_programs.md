# Airline group booking programs, group fare terms and group fare economics (Australia–Nepal carriers)

Research date: 20 November 2026. Method note: WebFetch could not reach any airline host in this session (DNS failure for malaysiaairlines.com, singaporeair.com, virginaustralia.com, jetstar.com, cxagents.com and qantas.com). Every finding below comes from search-engine extracts of the named pages, not from reading the full documents. Quoted wording is as the extract showed it. Verify exact wording against the original PDFs before relying on it commercially.

## Q1. Published group booking rules by carrier (minimum size, who can book, portals, booking window, quote validity, deposit, names, attrition, ticketing, free seats, baggage)

### Takeaway
Every carrier that publishes terms sets the economy group minimum at 10 passengers on the same flight. Beyond that, the terms differ widely. The most complete public terms are from Qantas (AU$200 deposit per person, 20% free attrition, non-refundable deposit), Singapore Airlines (IATA agents only, 10% deposit collected only on cancellation, names at 42 days), Jetstar (A$30–60 deposit per passenger per flight, names at 14 days) and Cathay Pacific (deadlines set in each contract, names at D-21). The Nepal-relevant Asian carriers mostly publish no deposit, attrition or free-seat terms at all: Malaysia Airlines, Batik Air, Thai, Air India, China Southern and Himalaya.

#### Malaysia Airlines (MH)
- Minimum of 10 passengers on the same flight, date and itinerary. "Bookings may only be made on the group booking portal by MHGroupbooking." The airline "reserves the right to approve or reject any application" and may cancel bookings or deny boarding if it detects abuse or fraud. Special meals need at least 48 hours' notice and group seat selection at least seven working days' notice. — [Malaysia Airlines group booking forms page](https://www.malaysiaairlines.com/my/en/group-booking-forms.html.html) (undated, current site)

Payment proof goes to anz.groups@malaysiaairlines.com, and payment is accepted only in AUD and NZD. Names go in by template upload or manual keying. The extract reads: "Click 'Submit' if names are subject to changes. Changes are allowed up to 48 hours before departure." The extract did not show deposit or balance timelines. — [MH Group Booking Portal Quick User Guide (Nov 2020)](https://www.mhmalaysiaexpert.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/MH-Group-Booking-Portal-Quick-User-Guide.pdf)
- The MH Australia agent clue card (Version 13, effective 28 July 2020) tells agents to "Observe the EARLIER auto ticketing time limit set by both Malaysia Airlines and other carrier in your PNR or fare expiry date whichever comes sooner." This is a general ticketing rule, not specific to groups. — [MH Australia Clue Card, 28 July 2020](https://www.mhmalaysiaexpert.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Malaysia-Airlines-MH-Australia-Clue-Card-effective-28-July-2020.pdf)

### Inferences
- For Australia–Nepal, a Qantas, Virgin Australia or Jetstar group would always be interline, since none of them flies to Kathmandu. Under Qantas's own terms, interline groups get a 45-day ticketing deadline and no name changes. That removes the main flexibility advantage of a group booking.
- In practice, group fares for this market come from the Asian hub carriers: MH, Batik (OD), SQ, TG, CX, AI and CZ. Most of them publish only the 10-passenger minimum and leave deposits, attrition and free seats to each quote. Commercial terms are therefore negotiated deal by deal and can't be modelled from published rules.
- SQ's structure (10% deposit charged only if seats are cancelled after the deposit deadline, names at 42 days) and Qantas's (AU$200 non-refundable, 20% free attrition) show the two common designs: a cancellation penalty versus an upfront deposit.
- Air India's rule that 10+ passengers on the same flight must go through the Group Desk, together with MH's "abuse or fraud" clause, means an app that pools families and books them as separate individual tickets on one flight could breach agent rules if an agency does the ticketing.

### Gaps
- None of the in-scope carriers publish group quote validity or how far ahead groups can be requested. The only related items are Qantas's three-working-day quote turnaround and Cathay's note that a "saved offer" holds no inventory.
- Not found: group baggage allowances for any carrier, beyond Qantas's "special baggage requirements ... may be eligible".
- Not found: official group terms for China Southern, Scoot, Thai (group-specific), Himalaya, and current Batik Air/Malaysia Airlines deposit, attrition and name rules. The MH portal guide and clue card are 2020.
- Not found: Cathay Australian-market terms, the Jetstar Australian point-of-sale deposit table, or VA's deposit amount and minimum size.
- Not found: fare validity rules (maximum stay, return-date flexibility) for group fares at any carrier.
- I did not verify which carriers currently serve Kathmandu nonstop from their hubs, for example whether Cathay Pacific currently operates to KTM.

## Q2. How do airlines price group requests? (revenue management, displacement, peak vs off-peak, festival dates, typical discounts)

### Takeaway
The revenue-management literature is consistent: a group request is priced at a floor equal to the revenue the airline would lose by displacing individual passengers. Off-peak, that cost is low and groups can be cheap. When flights are expected to sell out, it can exceed the individual fares the group members would otherwise pay. I found no primary or neutral industry source that quantifies a "typical" group discount, and no evidence that airlines discount groups on peak festival dates. The only quantified peak claim (groups about 9% above individual fares on peak dates) comes from a vendor blog citing a report I could not verify.

### Cited Findings
- Svrcek (MIT Flight Transportation Laboratory Report R91-5, May 1991) says "group passenger demand differs in several important respects from individual passenger demand". He presents a model "for determining the minimum per passenger fare an airline should charge an ad hoc group request based on the displacement of individual passengers", with leg-based and network displacement-cost models and an "N-Split Minimum Group Fare Model". — [Svrcek, MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/68097); [full text via CORE](https://core.ac.uk/download/4432395.pdf)
- Bid-price control, the standard modern O&D method: "A booking request for a passenger itinerary is rejected if the bid price exceeds the fare for the itinerary, and is accepted otherwise. For a multi-leg itinerary, the ODF fare must be greater than the sum of the bid prices of all flight legs used by the itinerary." — [McGill & van Ryzin, "Revenue Management: Research Overview and Prospects" (1999)](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/3925/vanryzin_revenue.pdf)
- Revenue management balances "spoilage, displacement, and diversion" costs. A booking is worth accepting when its yield exceeds what the airline could earn by holding the seats for later, higher-priced sales. — [Fedorco & Hospodka, Perner's Contacts](https://pernerscontacts.upce.cz/index.php/perner/article/download/729/563/1238)
- "Most literature on inventory control does not discuss policies for group arrivals." One paper applies bid-price control to group bookings, including cancellations and refunds, and reports higher total revenue than the traditional model. — [A network group booking model for airline revenue management, Journal of Modelling in Management 16(3)](https://www.emerald.com/jm2/article-abstract/16/3/861/434531/A-network-group-booking-model-for-airline-revenue?redirectedFrom=fulltext)
- A statistical RM model accepts a group request only if enough seats remain, then reduces remaining capacity by the group size g. — [A Statistical Modeling Approach to Airline Revenue Management](https://silo.tips/download/a-statistical-modeling-approach-to-airline-revenue-management)
- PROS Group Sales Optimizer, the main commercial group-pricing tool, "determines both the average marginal cost of providing the seats to the group and the marginal revenue curve of the group". It prices from "a continuous price curve, rather than from arbitrary, discrete price points". PROS claims a 15% increase in group revenue, a 25% higher conversion ratio, 60% faster quote turnaround, and "3X" group sales revenue at Japan Airlines. These are vendor marketing claims. — [PROS Group Sales Optimizer](https://pros.com/products/airline-group-sales-software/); [PROS GSO product guide](https://pros.com/learn/product-solution-information/group-sales-optimizer-transform-groups-business-dynamic-pricing/); [PROS marketplace](https://marketplace.pros.com/product-insights/group-sales-optimizer)
- A vendor-affiliated article reports a Middle Eastern carrier lifting its group "acceptance percentage from 8% to 29%" after automating group evaluation. The definition of acceptance (airline acceptance or customer conversion) is unclear. — [LinkedIn: How Airlines Can Increase Group Revenue by 28%](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-airlines-can-increase-group-revenue-28-shano-k-sam)
- Amadeus's group tool "Negotiated Space" lets airlines allocate a predefined number of seats to specific leisure agencies or TMCs under negotiated agreements. — [AltexSoft: airline RM software](https://www.altexsoft.com/blog/airline-revenue-management-software/)
- Airline terms show that group acceptance depends on capacity. Cathay: a saved group offer "is not honoured if the flight stops qualifying for group business" ([CX UAE terms Oct 2024](https://www.cxagents.com/content/dam/cathay-agents/ae/2024/Group%20terms%20and%20conditions_AE_Oct.pdf)). Malaysia Airlines "reserves the right to approve or reject any application" ([MH group page](https://www.malaysiaairlines.com/my/en/group-booking-forms.html.html)). Himalaya applies conditions "as per seat availability and seasonality" ([H9 FAQs](https://himalaya-airlines.com/contact/faqs)).
- A vendor blog says group fares "averaged about 9% higher than individual fares on peak dates", citing Business Travel Executive (2024). I could not find or verify the original report. — [futureflights.ai](https://futureflights.ai/efficient-group-flight-booking)
- Another blog cites Expedia: a group booked three months ahead saved 15%, while one booking a week out paid 10% more than solo travellers. Unverified, and the blog's own tables show group fares slightly above solo fares on some routes. — [futureflights.ai friends-trip](https://futureflights.ai/friends-trip-flights)
- Australian agency blogs say a group quote "may reflect the carrier's expected average fare for the route rather than the lowest fare currently visible online". Individual bookings can win during sales, because "promotional fares are usually sold to individual bookings". They advise comparing a group quote with individual fares including baggage, seat and change fees. I could not open these pages to confirm which blog says which. — [SkyTrips (AU)](https://skytrips.com.au/blog/group-vs-individual-flight-booking-price-comparison); [Travel Agent Finder (AU)](https://www.travelagentfinder.com.au/blog/group-airfares-are-they-really-cheaper)
- The fare-bucket mechanism: "If the cheapest bucket can't fit your whole group, everyone gets bumped to a higher-priced bucket – even when cheaper seats are still available for individual travelers." — [Dollar Flight Club](https://dollarflightclub.com/articles/book-flights-large-groups-without-overpaying/). Illustration: three seats reserved together cost $250 each versus $200 each when bought individually. — [Explore.com](https://www.explore.com/1389600/can-buying-airplane-tickets-individually-group-savings/)
- Festival-peak behaviour (Nepal domestic routes, not group-specific): "Airline Operators Increased Ticket Fares by Up to 68% for Dashain Travelers" ([Banking News, Sept 2024](https://bankingnews.com.np/2024/09/773/)); "Cheap air tickets disappear, leaving Dashain travelers with costly options" ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/business/cheap-air-tickets-disappear-leaving-dashain-travelers-with-costly-options/)); "Airline operators slash Dashain airfares after selling 90% tickets" ([Khabarhub, 2023](https://english.khabarhub.com/2023/17/322691/)).
- International Nepal routes at peak: Nepal's recruitment-agency association (NAFEA) objected to daily fare increases. It blamed a few airlines for "artificial seat shortages" that fed black-market sales, and said more than 1,500 workers fly daily from Nepal to Malaysia and the Middle East. — [myRepublica (mid-Nov; article ID suggests 2024)](https://myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/airfare-hike-puts-nepali-migrant-workers-at-disadvantage-6734344ebb8d5.html); [Ratopati](https://english.ratopati.com/story/36418)

### Inferences
- For Nepali families flying Australia to Kathmandu at Dashain/Tihar (usually late September to November) or over Australian Christmas school holidays, hub-to-KTM legs (KUL/SIN/BKK/DEL/CAN–KTM) are likely near full at high fares. Under displacement or bid-price pricing, the group floor on those dates would sit at or above the higher individual buckets, or the request would be declined. A two-leg itinerary must clear the bid price on both legs.
- Group discounts are therefore likely only on off-peak or shoulder dates. That is also when individual sale fares are most often available, so the group advantage is squeezed at both ends.
- The commonly repeated "groups save 10–20%" has no primary source. The report writer should treat any fixed group-discount percentage as unsupported.
- Large carriers use dynamic group-pricing tools (PROS GSO, and VA's FAQ titled "Group Sales Optimiser"), so group quotes track current demand forecasts rather than a fixed discount off published fares.

### Gaps
- I found no IATA, airline or academic source with empirical group-versus-individual fare comparisons by season, and no airline statement on group pricing for Dashain/Tihar or Australian peak periods.
- I could not access the full texts of Svrcek (1991) or the Journal of Modelling in Management paper, so the model results and any numbers are not captured.
- I could not verify the Business Travel Executive (2024) "9% higher on peak dates" figure at source.

## Q4. Real advantages of a group fare beyond price, and the risks

### Takeaway
The verifiable advantages are contractual. A price can be held months ahead for a small or deferred deposit (Qantas AU$200 per person; SQ 10% charged only if seats are cancelled; Jetstar A$30–60 per passenger per flight). Names can be given late (SQ 42 days, Cathay D-21, Jetstar 14 days). Qantas allows up to 20% of seats to be released without penalty, and some carriers offer free pre-seating. The risks are equally concrete: forfeited deposits, loss of the deposit if attrition exceeds the allowance, fixed-date non-refundable seats, automatic cancellation for missed deadlines, and no name changes on interline international groups.

### Cited Findings
**Advantages**
- Price held with a small deposit: Qantas AU$200 per person (international economy, Australian point of sale) due 14 days after confirmation ([QGT clue card](https://www.qantasgrouptravel.com/media/ly0hzw0u/posau-clue-card-international.pdf)). SQ's 10% deposit is "collected by SQ only when seats are cancelled via ADM", with free cancellation until the deposit deadline ([SQ IATA T&C](https://www.singaporeair.com/en_UK/gb/plan-travel/local-promotions/groups/IATA-Agents-TnC)). Jetstar charges AUD 30 (short-haul) or AUD 60 (long-haul) per passenger per flight ([Jetstar MY SEA T&C](https://jetstar.com/my/en/flights/group-bookings/south-east-asia-terms-and-conditions)). Virgin Australia says advance bookings "may be held with a small deposit" ([VA group travel](https://www.virginaustralia.com/au/en/travel-info/specific-travel/group-travel)).
- Late names: SQ 42 days before departure ([SQ](https://www.singaporeair.com/en_UK/gb/plan-travel/local-promotions/groups/IATA-Agents-TnC)); Cathay D-21 or per contract ([CX SAMEA 2026](https://www.cxagents.com/content/dam/cathay-agents/lk/2026/GROUP%20BOOKING%20TERMS%20AND%20CONDITIONS_AE_IN_BH_LK_21052026.pdf)); Jetstar 14 days, with free name changes up to 2 business days before departure ([Jetstar RoW](https://www.jetstar.com/us/en/flights/group-bookings/rest-of-world-group-bookings-terms-and-conditions)); Virgin Australia name changes up to 24–72 hours before departure depending on region ([VA Principle Terms](https://www.virginaustralia.com/content/dam/vaa/documents/specific-travel/groups-principle-terms-and-conditions.pdf)); MH portal changes "up to 48 hours before departure" (2020 guide) ([MH portal guide](https://www.mhmalaysiaexpert.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/MH-Group-Booking-Portal-Quick-User-Guide.pdf)).
- Free attrition: Qantas allows reductions "by up to 20% from the original group booking size" with the deposit applied to final payment. — [QGT clue card](https://www.qantasgrouptravel.com/media/ly0hzw0u/posau-clue-card-international.pdf)
- Seats together: Qantas offers free pre-seating within the group block up to four days before departure ([Qantas Select a Group Seat](https://qantas.com/agencyconnect/au/en/policies/book/seating/select-a-group-seat.html)). MH offers group seat selection with seven working days' notice ([MH group page](https://www.malaysiaairlines.com/my/en/group-booking-forms.html.html)).
- Services: Qantas groups "may be eligible" for assisted check-in, pre-seating and special baggage requirements. — [Qantas agencyconnect Group Travel](https://www.qantas.com/agencyconnect/us/en/support/agency-support/group-travel.html)
- One price for everyone: agency sources describe the main benefit as a single locked price across all passengers. — [SkyTrips (AU)](https://skytrips.com.au/blog/group-vs-individual-flight-booking-price-comparison)
- Free seats exist at some carriers for larger groups, for example Cathay MICE (UAE) gives one free economy ticket per 16. — [CX UAE MICE T&C Oct 2024](https://www.cxagents.com/content/dam/cathay-agents/ae/2024/Group%20terms%20and%20conditions_MICE_AE_Oct.pdf)

**Risks**
- Deposits forfeited: Qantas deposits have "No refund" and are lost if reductions exceed 20% ([QGT clue card](https://www.qantasgrouptravel.com/media/ly0hzw0u/posau-clue-card-international.pdf)). Jetstar deposits are "non-refundable and non-transferable" ([Jetstar MY SEA T&C](https://jetstar.com/my/en/flights/group-bookings/south-east-asia-terms-and-conditions)).
- Automatic cancellation: a Jetstar booking "will be cancelled automatically" if the deposit is late ([Jetstar RoW](https://www.jetstar.com/us/en/flights/group-bookings/rest-of-world-group-bookings-terms-and-conditions)). At Cathay, missing deposit requirements can trigger automatic release of the seats ([CX SAMEA 2026](https://www.cxagents.com/content/dam/cathay-agents/lk/2026/GROUP%20BOOKING%20TERMS%20AND%20CONDITIONS_AE_IN_BH_LK_21052026.pdf)).
- Inflexible seats: Batik Air group bookings are fixed-date, fixed-flight and non-refundable ([Lion Air Group support](https://lionairgroupsupport.freshdesk.com/en/support/solutions/articles/70000637303-international-group-booking)). SQ does not permit date changes on one-way group bookings ([SQ](https://www.singaporeair.com/en_UK/gb/plan-travel/local-promotions/groups/IATA-Agents-TnC)). Jetstar does not permit origin or destination changes ([Jetstar RoW](https://www.jetstar.com/us/en/flights/group-bookings/rest-of-world-group-bookings-terms-and-conditions)).
- No name changes on interline or international groups (Qantas), and an earlier 45-day ticketing deadline when an interline carrier is involved. — [QGT clue card](https://www.qantasgrouptravel.com/media/ly0hzw0u/posau-clue-card-international.pdf); [Qantas Group T&C](https://resource.qantasgrouptravel.com/pdfs/qfdsa.pdf); [Qantas SEA FAQ](https://www.jetstar.com/_/media/files/qantas-group/qantas-group-081-sea_faqs.pdf?_z=z)
- No-shows: Air India refunds "only statutory taxes" ([Air India](https://www.airindia.com/language-masters/en/book/group-ticket-booking.html)); Cathay does not allow a no-show passenger to be rebooked ([CX India](https://www.cxagents.com/content/dam/cathay-agents/in/documents/Group%20terms%20and%20conditions_IN_22Sep.pdf)).
- Quote not guaranteed until confirmed: Cathay's saved offer holds "NO Inventory" ([CX UAE Oct 2024](https://www.cxagents.com/content/dam/cathay-agents/ae/2024/Group%20terms%20and%20conditions_AE_Oct.pdf)). MH may reject any application ([MH](https://www.malaysiaairlines.com/my/en/group-booking-forms.html.html)).
- Change fees: Qantas charges AU$20 per person before full payment and AU$175 per person after. — [QGT clue card](https://www.qantasgrouptravel.com/media/ly0hzw0u/posau-clue-card-international.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a group-buying app, the real product is risk transfer: a held price plus late names. If the app holds the group contract, it carries the attrition risk. Families dropping out above the allowance (Qantas: 20%) costs the deposit. Unsold seats after the deposit deadline cost 10% at SQ (charged through the agent) or the whole deposit elsewhere.
- Nepali families' travel dates and departure cities (SYD/MEL/BNE/PER/ADL) vary, so getting 10+ people on the same flight and date is a hard constraint. Qantas's "20+ to a common destination for a single event" definition is the only published exception found, and Qantas doesn't serve KTM.
- Low or deferred deposits make it cheap to hold seats early. The economic value is an option on a fixed price, and that is worth most for peak dates, which is exactly when airlines are least willing to quote groups cheaply (Q2).

### Gaps
- No carrier published penalties per unsold seat after ticketing, apart from deposit forfeiture and SQ's 10%. Cathay, MH, Thai, Air India and Batik attrition scales were not found.
- No group baggage uplift was found for any carrier, which matters for families carrying gifts and goods to Nepal.

## Q5. Do consolidators, wholesalers or Nepal GSAs sell net/private fares to Nepal that beat public fares, and how could a small operator access them?

### Takeaway
Australia has several large air consolidators supplying net and private fares to agencies: Helloworld's Air Tickets, Australian Travel Consolidation and CVFR. Airline GSAs handle group requests for some Nepal-route carriers; Batik Air's ANZ groups go to an airline representation company. However, no source shows Kathmandu net fares beating public fares. Access is through the travel-agency trade (GDS/consolidator accounts, and IATA accreditation for some group desks), not open to the public.

### Cited Findings
- Air Tickets (Helloworld) supplies fares to agencies across Australia, New Zealand and overseas OTAs, with ticketing for more than 100 international airlines. In January 2025 the Australian Travel Agents Co-operative (ATAC) appointed Air Tickets as its exclusive air consolidator in a three-year deal. — [Karryon (Jan 2025)](https://karryon.com.au/industry-news/atac-chooses-air-tickets/); [Helloworld consolidation brands](https://www.helloworldlimited.com.au/consolidation-brands/)
- Australian Travel Consolidation (ATC) says it consolidates over 100 airlines, including Thai Airways, and offers "Private Airfares" to Australian agents. These are self-reported claims. — [ATC](https://atconsolidation.com.au/)
- CVFR Consolidation Services calls itself "the fastest growing Consolidation business in Australia", with tools for net and private fares and an "Asia Travel Network". — [CVFR Group](https://cvfrgroup.com/cvfr-consolidation-services)
- How net fares work: airlines and consolidators negotiate net (bulk, consolidator or private) fares "for blocks of seats which carriers consider hard to fill". They are restricted from online display, and agents access them through a GDS, NDC or consolidator platforms. — [AltexSoft: Air consolidator](https://www.altexsoft.com/blog/airline-consolidator/); [Sky Bird Travel](https://skybirdtravel.com/)
- "There is no universal discount percentage for consolidator fares"; the fare depends on airline, route, cabin, inventory, dates and the commercial agreement. — [CEO Flights (2026)](https://www.ceoflights.com/blog/consolidator-fares-explained-2026/)
- With net fares the agent sets its own markup, whereas published fares pay a set commission. Consolidator fares often carry more restrictions, for example on credits, advance seat selection or changes. — [AltexSoft](https://www.altexsoft.com/blog/airline-consolidator/); [software.travel](https://www.software.travel/blog/tips/airline-consolidators-reviews/)
- Group desks restrict access: SQ accepts group requests only from "Agencies with IATA accreditation" ([SQ](https://www.singaporeair.com/en_UK/gb/plan-travel/local-promotions/groups/IATA-Agents-TnC)). Cathay's group tools are agent-facing ([CX ZA 2026](https://www.cxagents.com/content/dam/cathay-agents/za/documents/2026/GROUP%20BOOKING%20TERMS%20AND%20CONDITIONS_ZA_IATA_21052026.pdf)). MH group bookings must go through its portal ([MH](https://www.malaysiaairlines.com/my/en/group-booking-forms.html.html)).
- GSA channels for Nepal-route carriers: Batik Air's ANZ group requests go to batikairgroups@airlinerepservices.com ([Lion Air Group support](https://lionairgroupsupport.freshdesk.com/en/support/solutions/articles/70000637303-international-group-booking)). Malindo appointed Yeti Incentive Travels as its Nepal GSA in 2015, which may be outdated ([Spotlight Nepal, 2015](https://www.spotlightnepal.com/2015/01/30/malindo-air-to-fly-to-nepal-from-feb-6)). Air India issued an RFP for a passenger GSA in Nepal ([Air India RFP](https://dmlib.airindia.com/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:ce573c5f-e824-47b2-a33d-f5ad1bb34d46/original/as/rfp-for-pax-gsa-ra-nepal-ai-ix.pdf)). Lufthansa Group appointed Society International as offline GSA for Nepal ([B360 Nepal](https://www.b360nepal.com/detail/24728/lufthansa-appoints-society-international-as-offline-gsa-for-nepal)). Gandaki Travels says it represents GSAs of leading international airlines ([Gandaki Travels](https://gandakitravels.com/about-us)).
- A TravelAgentMall article (promotional) says agents find airline consolidators "the best source of discounted group fares". Another source advises taking a consolidator deal only if it is "significantly cheaper", because published fares often have better terms. — [search extracts; TravelAgentMall](https://www.travelagentmall.com/)

### Inferences
- A small operator (the app) could reach net or private fares to Kathmandu in two ways. It could partner with or become an Australian travel agency holding a consolidator account (Air Tickets, ATC, CVFR), or it could work through a host agency with IATA accreditation to reach SQ and CX group desks. Consumers and non-agents cannot get these fares directly. This is inferred from the agent-only distribution described above. I did not find the specific accreditation requirements (for example ATAS) in this research.
- Because net fares are for seats "carriers consider hard to fill", they are most likely on off-peak dates. That is the same pattern as group pricing, so consolidators are unlikely to unlock cheap peak-festival seats.

### Gaps
- No source lists consolidator net fares to KTM from Australia or compares them with public fares.
- Current Nepal GSAs for MH, SQ, TG, CZ and Batik were not confirmed. The directory and press items are old or undated.
- Australian travel-agency accreditation and licensing requirements, and consolidator onboarding criteria, were not researched.
